Draft · not confirmed

Draft, a clear no: I rejected generic suggestion chips ("create a campaign", "automate a flow") because they felt personal to no one. I pushed for suggestions personalized to each user and got closer, but not all the way: at launch, suggestions are relevant to the page the user is on, so the flows page recommends how to use flows and the campaigns page how to use campaigns. The principle: keep people in the work they planned for the day. The next step is for Composer to take the lead, for example pointing to a campaign after auditing a flow.

Draft · not confirmed

Draft, a real moment: Customers said an email used the wrong logo, because Composer pulled from a logo they had updated years ago. I traced it to that source and prioritized on the roadmap a view that shows users where Composer pulls its information from and where to update it. I advocated for it and did not design it; it shipped while I was on leave. Not yet built: letting Composer fix the source itself. [verify public-safe]

How I defined quality

Draft · not confirmed

Draft: I set concrete output criteria for the first release: no emojis, tables rendered as tables, and a short list of recommendations (three at a time, more on request) rather than a never-ending one; when shown in line, one item at a time with arrows to browse. I read real customer conversations and eval results, especially the frustrated ones. The three-at-a-time rule was my advocacy and may not be in the product yet [verify before stating it as shipped].
