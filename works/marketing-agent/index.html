Marketing Agent

Lead Designer, working with engineering on Klaviyo's Marketing Agent, an AI agent that generates campaigns, forms, and flows for marketers. I took it from prototype to its launch at K:BOS 2025 and designed the jumpstart onboarding for new customers.

Step 1 of 9

Context

A team of designers shaped the product vision and the happy path. I owned the systems underneath like how a new customer gets into Marketing Agent from Klaviyo sign-up, what happens when it gets something wrong, and what happens when someone wants to edit what it made. I worked through each of those with engineering until they shipped.

I started in May 2025. It launched at K:BOS, Klaviyo's 2025 conference in Boston.

Launch scope

At launch it generated content for campaigns, forms, and flows. There were two ways in.

Existing customers saw recommendations in line, in the table on the campaigns, forms, or flows page, marked "we built this for you." A second entry point sat in the top nav.

New customers got a jumpstart path. They signed up from klaviyo.com, entered their website, and went through an onboarding that captured their brand colors, logo, and brand voice. We used those to curate a first set of campaigns, forms, and flows.

Step 2 of 9

Problem and stakes

Marketers spent hours building content and often didn't know what to send next. We set out to give brands new campaign ideas and get them to their own customers faster.

Step 3 of 9

Customer voice

I talked to customers about every other day, testing prototypes and designs. I used self-service recruiting to get real users in front of the work quickly.

They wanted to see where content came from

Existing customers asked where Klaviyo pulled their information from. We added a link in the agent to the source, the brand and colors area of the product.

They wanted control

Most of the time, customers edited what we generated. They almost never just clicked send. I took that into Composer.

They used it where they already worked

Existing customers had two entry points, and in-line recommendations got far more adoption than the header button.

Step 4 of 9

Constraints

A fixed launch date

We were launching at K:BOS and new AI features and abilities came out every day. We had to decide what would make it into our product on launch day.

Content quality

Customers kept telling us the generated content didn't match what they wanted. Because we pulled from brand settings, the interface sent them there to fix it when it didn't match their expectations.

Editing

Customers wanted to land directly in their Klaviyo editor. The backend couldn't support that at the time of launch, so we designed and built an intermediate step for users to view the generations and decide if they wanted to keep the generated content as a draft or not.

Step 5 of 9

Decisions

Decision 1: the agent proposes, the customer decides

The agent never acted on its own. Nothing was created in a customer's account until they chose. Clicking 'Edit' created a draft in the customer's account while clicking 'Send' created and sent the object in a customer's account.

Decision 2: an intermediate screen instead of the editor

Customers wanted to go straight into the editor, and the backend couldn't open it directly. We had to ship for K:BOS, so I designed an intermediate screen that shows what the agent generated and offers two choices: send it now, or click edit, which created a draft object for them in their account.

Decision 3: pull what we can, ask only when we must

Leadership wanted a fast start for new customers, so I took on the full flow for getting started with Klaviyo using the Marketing Agent.

I worked through it one-on-one with an engineer, sorting every step into two piles: what we could pull or assume from the customer's website, and what the customer had to confirm. If the site ran on Shopify and we pulled that information, for example, we skipped the question about where it was hosted. We asked only when we couldn't tell. Every decision in the onboarding went that way: automate it unless we couldn't.

Step 6 of 9

When the AI was wrong

Marketing Agent could have learned from what a customer actually sends. It pulled from Klaviyo's brand settings instead, and that's where it broke.

The two-year-old logo

Some customers got a generated campaign with their logo from two years ago, which was stressful to see. They had updated the logo in their recent campaigns and recent email templates but never in brand settings. Most customers at Klaviyo clone an old template and never opened the brand settings section. Our generation resource was a place in the product where customers didn't frequent.

The Marketing Agent had to be designed to point customers back to the brand settings so they could update it and correct future generations.

Step 7 of 9

How I defined quality

We defined good as matching the customer's brand. Customers liked the ideas the Marketing Agent came up with but when the colors, logo, or voice were off, for example, they didn't count it as a good product.

How we checked it

We added thumbs up and thumbs down on generations and tracked how often people clicked Send versus Edit. We also talked with customers often about the generations and their improvement overtime.

Step 8 of 9

Outcome

Marketing Agent launched at K:BOS 2025. The jumpstart path got new customers to ready-to-send content quickly, and in-line recommendations drew far more use than the header entry point.

A few lessons that I learned from this product include:

  • Meet people in the work they're already doing. In-line beat the header, so show recommendations on the page where the work happens.
  • Let the user lead. Marketing Agent chose what to make, and customers want more help doing that compared to the creative part.
  • Quality before autonomy. If fixing the output takes longer than writing it themselves, people stop using it.
Step 9 of 9

What I'd do differently

I'd push much earlier to learn from customers' past campaigns in generations. Klaviyo knows customers clone old campaigns and templates when they send, so I'd start from those templates and generate only the content and pull in images. That likely costs Klaviyo less than generating everything from scratch, and it gets around the brand problems.
