Context

I was the main designer working with engineering on Marketing Agent. A team of designers helped visualize the product and create its happy path; I focused on the systems level.

That meant thinking through how new customers would get involved with Marketing Agent right from Klaviyo sign-up, what would happen when it didn't work, and what would happen when a user wanted to edit something. I worked through those use cases with engineering to get them shipped.

I started on Marketing Agent in May 2025, and it launched at K:BOS, Klaviyo's 2025 conference in Boston.

Launch scope

At launch, Marketing Agent focused on content generation rather than data. It generated campaigns, forms, and flows for customers, and there were two ways in.

Existing customers saw recommendations in line, in the table on the campaigns, forms, or flows page, saying "we built this for you." A second entry point sat in the top nav header. Customers sometimes discovered it, but the in-line recommendations got far more adoption.

New customers got a jumpstart path, built to get them up and running on Klaviyo as quickly as possible. They signed up from the login page on Klaviyo's website and went through a specialized onboarding that captured their brand colors, logo, and brand voice, which we used to curate generated campaigns, forms, and flows for them. This path worked really well: it didn't need much data about the customer, and the content was ready to go.

Problem and stakes

Marketers were spending hours building content, and they often didn't have ideas for what to send next. So we focused on helping brands come up with new campaign ideas and get them out to their customers quickly.

Customer voice

I talked to customers almost every other day, testing different prototypes and designs, and used self-service recruiting to get real users in front of them.

Showing where the content came from

Existing customers really wanted to know where Klaviyo was pulling information from to create their content. So we added a link in Marketing Agent that showed the source: the brand and colors area of the product.

Customers always wanted control

In my experience, about 95% of the time a customer wanted to edit whatever we created for them. They almost never just clicked send. Customers always wanted control, and that's a learning I took with me into Composer.

Placement

Marketing Agent had two entry points for existing customers: the top nav header, and recommendations in line, inside the tables where customers already work. We didn't set out to test them against each other, but usage told the story: in-line recommendations got far more adoption than the header [FILL IN: in-line lift over the header, as a percentage]. Constraints

Three constraints shaped the design most.

A fixed launch date

We wanted to launch at K:BOS and keep up with the AI race.

Content quality

We kept hearing that the quality of the generated content wasn't matching what customers wanted. In the UX, we pointed customers to the source, for example asking them to update their brand and colors, because that's where we pulled from. We also advocated for tracking the changes users made when editing, though that wasn't implemented.

Editing

Customers always wanted to edit, and to be taken straight to their Klaviyo editor, but the backend architecture couldn't support that. So we sent users to an intermediate screen showing what we'd generated for them, where they could send it right away or click Edit to open the editor.

Decisions

Decision 1: the agent proposes, the customer decides

The agent never acted on its own. It proposed content, and nothing was created in the customer's account until they chose what to do with it: clicking Edit created a draft, and clicking Send created a full campaign. Since customers wanted control almost every time, the customer always made the call.

Decision 2: an intermediate screen instead of the editor

Customers wanted to go straight into their Klaviyo editor, but the backend architecture couldn't open it directly, and we needed to ship for K:BOS. So instead of a full preview with options to revise or roll back, we built an intermediate screen showing what the agent had generated, with two choices: send it right away, or click Edit to open it in the editor. What we gave up was a seamless edit.
