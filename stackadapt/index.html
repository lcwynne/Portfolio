Results16 / 26

Results

What happened, and what I learned.

  • Content generation shipped first, because that's what we were ready for, and it was used heavily.
  • Users enjoyed the data analysis features more than content generation.
  • Customers kept telling us they wanted the data behind a result before more copy.
  • And they kept asking for more and more: from analyzing, to creating, to personalization.

Exact metrics are confidential.

Results17 / 26

Evals

Stop relying on thumbs up and thumbs down.

  • Read the sentiment in the chats themselves: what customers like and dislike, without asking them.
  • In the future: a continuous loop with data scientists, so that signal improves the model automatically, not through direct edits.
  • That loop is also how the chat gets personalized for each customer.

How I operated

Leadership

  • Presented weekly to our CEO, CTO, CMO, and more for about a year and a half, answering questions live.
  • Presented the vision weekly to our VP of Design, and to the board.

The team

  • Started with a co-designer, then two more designers joined.
  • Designed the first widgets, then delegated widget consistency to my co-designer.
  • Stayed on the chat foundation, discoverability, and alerts and notifications, then delegated those once the foundational work was laid.
How I operated19 / 26

What I'd do differently

  1. Get ahead of the teams building agents: bring them together early, and ideate with them.
  2. Start with the data analysis.
  3. Treat cost per generation as a design constraint from day one.
